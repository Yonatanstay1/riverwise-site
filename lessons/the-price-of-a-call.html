LESSON 04 · POKER MATH · 5 MIN

The price of a call

Turn pot odds into a simple question you can actually answer.

Start with the final pot

Suppose the pot is 100 and an opponent bets 50. You must call 50 to contest a final pot of 200: the original 100, their 50 and your 50. Your break-even equity is 50 divided by 200, or 25%.

Threshold versus actual equity

Pot odds give you the minimum share of the pot you need in a simplified call decision. They do not tell you your actual equity. Actual equity depends on your hand, the board and the range of hands the opponent could hold. A range is a collection of plausible hands, not a single guess.

Know the assumptions

The simple calculation assumes no future betting and ignores rake. On earlier streets, future bets, implied odds and the risk of making a second-best hand can matter. Tournament risk can also change a decision. Use the toolkit calculator to see how bet size changes the threshold.

TAKE THIS WITH YOURequired equity = call amount ÷ final pot after your call. Compare this threshold with a reasoned estimate, not a hope.
