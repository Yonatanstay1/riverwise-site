LESSON 05 · BETTING DECISIONS · 5 MIN

Bet with a reason

Distinguish value, bluffs and the option to keep the pot small.

Value and bluffing

A value bet aims to be called by worse hands. A bluff aims to make better hands fold. Before you bet, name a few hands you expect to call or fold. A bet can deny equity too, but that does not remove the need to consider the opponent’s range.

Let the board guide you

A dry board has few connected or same-suit cards. A connected, two-suit board can offer more draws. Board texture, position, stack depth and prior action affect the range and size of sensible bets. Larger is not automatically better.

Checking is a real decision

Checking can keep weaker hands in, protect your checking range or avoid building a pot with a marginal hand. In other spots it may give a free card. The best choice depends on the situation; a single result cannot prove the decision was right or wrong.

TAKE THIS WITH YOUBefore betting, finish this sentence: “I expect these worse hands to call,” or “I expect these better hands to fold.”
